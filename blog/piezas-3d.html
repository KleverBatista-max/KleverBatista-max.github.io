El Slow 'N Sear: famoso en el mundo de los asados

El Slow 'N Sear es una canasta para carbón que va en el centro de una parrilla de tipo kettle y crea dos zonas de cocción: fuego directo de un lado y calor suave del otro, para cocinar «low and slow» y sellar al final. Lo creó David Parrish, de SnS Grills (antes Adrenaline Barbecue Company), hacia 2014, y la empresa adoptó el nombre Slow 'N Sear en 2019. Se hizo tan popular que hoy es casi un estándar entre quienes asan en serio, con modelos para las parrillas Weber de 18, 22 y 26 pulgadas.

Mi asador no es de esas medidas, así que dibujé mi versión a la medida exacta del mío.

Cómo lo hago

Mido, dibujo en Fusion 360 o AutoCAD, exporto a STL o 3MF e imprimo. Las versiones «v2» existen porque la primera casi nunca encaja a la primera.

3D parts

When something doesn't exist to fit my house, I draw it and print it. It's the same productive laziness as everything else: I'd rather model a part once than put up with the annoyance forever.

What's there

  • Cable holder and cable cover.
  • Outlet and charger covers. The charger one was born because I was tired of my charger getting stolen: it fits an Apple power adapter and has a slot for a padlock.
  • Hallway and dining-room covers (version 2), in Fusion 360. I kept the editable files, not just the print-ready part.
  • My own Slow 'N Sear, drawn in AutoCAD to the exact measurements of my grill.

The Slow 'N Sear: famous in the grilling world

The Slow 'N Sear is a charcoal basket that sits in the center of a kettle-style grill and creates two cooking zones: direct fire on one side and gentle heat on the other, for cooking "low and slow" and searing at the end. It was created by David Parrish of SnS Grills (formerly Adrenaline Barbecue Company) around 2014, and the company took the Slow 'N Sear name in 2019. It became so popular that it's almost a standard among serious grillers, with models for 18, 22 and 26-inch Weber kettles.

My grill isn't one of those sizes, so I drew my own version to the exact measurements of mine.

How I do it

I measure, draw in Fusion 360 or AutoCAD, export to STL or 3MF and print. The "v2" versions exist because the first one almost never fits.

See them in 3D

GitHub shows .stl files in a 3D viewer you can rotate. They are in the piezas-3d repository, MIT licensed: print and modify them.
